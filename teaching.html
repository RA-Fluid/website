Teaching & Mentorship

Connecting Physical Understanding, Engineering Design and Evidence

I teach from the premise that students develop durable understanding when mathematical models are connected to observable physical behaviour and then evaluated through computation or experiment. In engineering, equations and software outputs can easily become detached from the systems they represent. I therefore move deliberately among physical explanation, mathematical formulation, digital design, simulation, visual evidence, and experimental measurement.

My aim is not to reduce technical depth. It is to help students identify assumptions, predict trends, evaluate uncertainty, and determine whether a result is physically credible and fit for purpose. I teach computer-aided design and simulation as engineering decision-making tools, not simply as software skills.

My teaching is organised around three core principles:

  • Make the physics visible
  • Make engineering reasoning assessable
  • Provide feedback while students can still act on it

These principles support rigorous learning while making disciplinary expectations more transparent. Students may begin with different levels of mathematical, computational, design, or laboratory experience, but every student should be able to recognise what quality engineering work requires and how to progress towards it.

Learning Design in Practice

Make the Physics Visible

I use diagrams, demonstrations, high-speed imaging, measured datasets, simulations, and limiting cases to make the physical meaning of engineering equations visible. Visualisation provides a physical reference against which mathematical predictions can be tested.

Connect Design, Analysis and Manufacture

In project-based activities, I use a complete design cycle: requirements → concepts → CAD → analysis → manufacture → testing → redesign. Students learn to use CAD, simulation, and experimentation as connected sources of evidence.

Use Computation with Judgement

Attractive visual output can conceal poor assumptions. I train students to evaluate computational results critically: Are boundary conditions defensible? Is the solution mesh-independent? The goal is to develop engineers who use computation with critical confidence.

Make Feedback Actionable

I stage larger assignments so students receive early feedback on problem definition, preliminary models, and verification before the final submission, shifting feedback into a practical part of the learning process.

Assessment and Generative AI

Generative AI changes how evidence of learning should be designed. In open tasks, AI may be used where its purpose and limitations are explicit. Assessments ask students to critique AI-generated models, test predictions against conservation laws, and document corrections.

Inclusive and Responsible Education

Having studied and worked in Iran, Canada, and Australia, I recognise that students enter the classroom with different starting points. I provide multiple entry points into a concept—equations, physical demonstrations, and digital models—while preserving common academic standards.

Research Training & Supervision

My supervision approach combines clear research questions and milestones with early, actionable feedback and progressively greater researcher independence. My experience includes co-supervising MSc and Honours researchers, guiding visiting PhD scholars, and mentoring undergraduate interns.

Training covers the full lifecycle of a project: literature analysis, CAD, rapid prototyping, surface characterisation, optical flow measurement (PIV), mathematical modelling, and scientific writing.

Student Project Development

I am particularly interested in guiding undergraduate, Honours, and postgraduate projects that connect engineering science with digital and physical implementation. Suitable projects span:

  • Simulation-led mechanical design & advanced manufacturing
  • Functional-surface fabrication & interfacial transport
  • Optical flow measurement & experimental instrumentation
  • Complex and non-Newtonian fluids
  • CFD, FEA, and computational model development

Engineering Education for Professional Practice

My goal is to help students move beyond technically correct calculations towards defensible engineering decisions. I view teaching as an evidence-based and iterative practice. By evaluating recurring patterns of misconception, peer review, and students’ ability to transfer methods to unfamiliar problems, I continuously revise my examples and assessment strategies.

My objective is to develop engineers who can connect physical principles with digital tools and experimental evidence, use emerging technologies responsibly, and communicate uncertainty with professional judgement.
